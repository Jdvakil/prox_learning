1. THE ARGUMENT TO BUILD

Use the author's question as the organizing principle:

  Does live proximity sensing improve collision avoidance in learned manipulation
  policies when task-relevant hazards are unavailable or ambiguous in RGB?

Separate two cases within that question: hazards whose direct visual appearance is
unavailable, and hazards that remain visible but whose local clearance is difficult
to estimate. They motivate the same sensing approach but require distinct evidence.

The central distinction should be:

  RGB provides visual task context; proximity supplies direct local range
  measurements. PACT learns how to combine these complementary observations when
  predicting manipulation actions.

Metric range is measured; the policy representation is learned. Proximity does not
directly measure a complete scene model, semantic context, or the correct action.
Use "visual task context" rather than always calling camera information "global":
the hallway policy has only a wrist camera. Distributed sensor viewpoints matter
alongside the difference between appearance and range.

Recommended title:
  PACT: Learning Collision-Aware Manipulation with Proximity Skin

More tightly scoped alternative:
  PACT: Proximity Skin for Collision-Aware Manipulation in Confined Spaces

Avoid an unqualified "invisible obstacles" title for the current readout results.
Keep visual unobservability prominent in the research question and explain exactly
which experimental condition implements it.

2. REPLACEMENT OPENING FOR PAPER.md

Manipulation requires both understanding the task and estimating clearance around
the moving robot. RGB observations provide rich visual task context, but nearby
surfaces can be occluded, outside the available camera views, or visually ambiguous.
In confined workspaces such as fume hoods, the arm must negotiate this geometry
while reaching, grasping, and transporting an object. Distributed proximity sensing
offers a complementary observation: local range measurements around the arm.

We investigate whether providing these measurements during policy execution can
reduce collisions in learned manipulation. We introduce PACT, which augments an
ACT policy with proximity features from 40 simulated sensors distributed along
the arm. PACT-readout encodes each sensor's recent depth observations using a
shared geometry encoder and jointly finetunes its features with the action policy.
The resulting policy combines visual context, robot joint state, and local range
information when predicting action chunks.

Our experiments distinguish collision avoidance from task completion. Across two
hallway evaluation sets, PACT-readout reduces hazard-contact rates from 34% to 12%
and from 40% to 18% relative to ACT, while observed placement rates increase from
28% to 40% and from 30% to 42%. The placement differences remain statistically
uncertain. An archived experiment separately examines a hazard excluded from RGB
rendering, and a spaced-bench evaluation identifies limits to the benefit across
tasks. Together, these studies examine how proximity sensing can complement visual
policies, with the strongest current readout evidence coming from confined hallway
manipulation.

For the submitted abstract, compress this opening and give exact evaluation-set
details in Methods. The archived study must retain its status and different model
identity wherever its quantitative results appear.

3. GIVE EACH EXPERIMENT A ROLE IN THE ARGUMENT

A. What local geometry is available from the skin? [S]

Use the sensor layout and recorded sensor-only reconstruction to make the sensing
contribution tangible. Show an actual 8x8 observation, its local range structure,
and how distributed viewpoints sample nearby surfaces.

The 20.62 mm encoder result is mean Euclidean endpoint error on valid targets.
It evaluates the encoder's auxiliary geometry prediction; it is not sensor accuracy,
robot clearance error, or the accuracy of the final finetuned policy representation.
Keep the physical sensor model, raw point reconstruction, encoder pretraining, and
policy performance as separate quantities.

B. Does proximity help in a controlled camera-hidden condition? [O-INV]

The archived visibility study is directly relevant to the author's question and
deserves a named supporting subsection: "Controlled visual unobservability."
Do not leave it buried among unrelated unsuccessful development experiments.

                        ACT contacts   PACT-raw contacts   Observed difference
  No bar                30/50 (60%)    29/50 (58%)         2 percentage points
  Visible bar           32/50 (64%)    25/50 (50%)        14 percentage points
  Camera-hidden bar     33/50 (66%)    20/50 (40%)        26 percentage points

All are the archived collision metric, which includes background contacts; these
are not hazard-specific bar-hit counts. Hidden-condition Fisher p = 0.01577.
Hidden-condition task success is 18/50 for ACT and 15/50 for raw. Report it alongside
contacts. "No significant success difference" does not establish equivalence.

The largest observed contact-rate difference occurs in the camera-hidden condition.
Describe this as consistent with the complementary-sensing motivation. Do not infer
a statistically established effect of visibility from a significant comparison in
one condition and a nonsignificant comparison in another; that needs an interaction
analysis. High background contact rates also limit mechanistic interpretation.

Explain the intervention plainly: geometry is excluded from policy RGB rendering,
retained in proximity rendering, and retained in collision physics. This is a
controlled simulator visibility condition, not a physical glass experiment.

This study used an older PACT-raw design and a different task. Source demonstrations
and trained checkpoints are no longer available; saved evaluation JSONs survive.
It supplies historical supporting evidence, not a reproducible readout replication.

C. Does the learned fusion method reduce contact during manipulation? [H-A, H-B]

These are the main PACT-readout results. Lead with the repeated 22-percentage-point
hazard-contact reduction across the two comparisons. Relative reductions are about
65% and 55%. Keep percentages and counts together and keep the protocols separate.

  H-A: ACT 17/50 bar contacts; raw 18/50; readout 6/50.
       Placement: ACT 14/50; raw 21/50; readout 20/50.
       Successful placement without counted collisions, derived from episode rows:
       ACT 13/50; raw 17/50; readout 20/50.

  H-B: ACT 20/50 bar contacts; raw 13/50; readout 9/50.
       Placement: ACT 15/50; raw 21/50; readout 21/50.
       Successful placement without counted collisions:
       ACT 12/50; raw 19/50; readout 19/50.

H-A is the historical random-house evaluation. H-B uses fixed house 1 and evaluation
seeds 2026-2075, with ACT training seed 1 and PACT training seed 0. The hallway bar is
not excluded from RGB rendering. These results establish observed contact reductions
in this setting, without isolating camera unobservability as the cause.

Use H-C as an additional evaluation of the historical readout checkpoint, not a
third independent training replication. Do not pool the three sets into an n=150
result or write that all three have their own matched ACT baseline.

D. How does the choice of representation affect the comparison? [H-A, H-B]

Explain raw versus readout as a comparison of complete proximity front ends.
Readout has fewer observed bar contacts in both tables; its separation from raw
is stronger in H-A and uncertain in H-B. Keep the design comparison explicit:
scalar versus learned features, snapshot versus history, 50 cm versus 20 cm cap,
320 versus 40 tokens, and joint encoder finetuning versus no learned skin encoder.
The present results do not isolate finetuning or any one of these differences.

E. Where does the benefit stop? [T-107]

Use a concise subsection titled "Task dependence" or "Extension to a spaced bench."
This is a completed result, not a pending run. Placement is ACT 13/50, raw 0/50,
readout 2/50; successful placement without counted collisions is 7/50, 0/50, 2/50.

Suggested interpretation:
  The contact reduction observed in the hallway did not extend to improved
  manipulation on the spaced bench. Additional proximity observations therefore
  do not provide a uniform task-performance benefit under the evaluated recipes.

Do not use the failure as proof that cameras already supplied enough geometry,
that the encoder saturated, or that proximity is useful only for collision avoidance.
Those are hypotheses. This experiment identifies scope and motivates further work.

Keep T-1011d's completed raw-only conditions, incomplete wrist-only run, OOD
diagnostics, and trained-only checkpoints in separately labeled inventory sections.

4. THE MISSING LINK FOR THE WORD "LIVE"

The saved runs establish that PACT receives proximity during inference. They do not
yet isolate how much of the benefit depends on its current proximity observations,
as opposed to the complete training recipe and learned policy differences.

If further experiment time is available, prioritize a targeted inference-input
study over another unrelated environment. This is a proposed study, not a result:

  - Freeze one trained readout policy and its encoder.
  - Use the same initial scenarios and evaluation protocol across conditions.
  - Compare live readings with a controlled stale or mismatched-reading condition.
  - Preserve sensor order, units, history shape, and preprocessing. Explain the
    disturbance; corrupted readings can themselves induce distribution shift.
  - Report contacts, task completion, and their joint outcome for every condition.

Separately, a paired action-query diagnostic can hold RGB, joint state, and policy
weights exactly fixed while changing only proximity. That checks input dependence;
action changes alone do not establish collision avoidance. During full rollouts,
RGB and joint state will naturally diverge as policies take different actions.

For the exact "unavailable to RGB" question, either use a controlled visibility
condition for the same model or audit hazard visibility over the relevant approach
history and all policy cameras. One occluded frame does not establish that the policy
never previously observed the hazard or that no indirect visual cues remain.

Do not call a zeroed-skin readout checkpoint "ACT." Retain independently trained ACT
as its own baseline. The older v1 skin-zeroing result does not establish dependence
for the current readout model.

5. CONCRETE REVISIONS TO THE NEW PAPER.md

20 cm encoder preprocessing cap from the simulated sensors' raw depth outputs.

Section 2: replace "camera never sees" and "invisible until it is hit" with the
specific observability conditions being studied. Replace "does it crash less" with
the author's research question. Depth grids carry local surface-shape information;
remove the claim that a sensor does not know shape. Replace claims that the network
uses a modality only when demonstrations cannot be explained without it with a
design hypothesis about learning to use complementary inputs.

Contributions: use three concrete items: distributed range sensing and its data
pipeline; geometry representation and policy fusion; an empirical investigation
of contact reduction and task dependence. Describe sensor verification accurately
(38/40 pass the stated check); do not call all 40 sensors verified. Present measured
findings as contributions, with completed negative results integrated into scope.

Section 3: label the inference diagram "one policy query," not one control step.
The reported default history comprises eight query observations, not consecutive
control steps. Branch the data diagram from native demonstrations to encoder
pretraining and separately to ACT conversion; pretraining uses native and pooled
input modes, not solely the converted ACT HDF5 path.

Sections 4-7: retain the accessible method explanation and apply the factual checks
listed below. For every component explain its purpose before listing dimensions.
Clarify that the policy learns from raw sensor-derived features, not the accumulated
world-coordinate point cloud shown in the illustrative reconstruction figure.

Sections 8-10: organize around the questions in section 3 of this review. Use
descriptive experiment names in prose, with H-A/H-B/etc. in table captions and source
notes. Replace "strongest table" with a descriptive protocol label. Replace "each
needs newer runs before it can carry a claim" with actual completion/evidence status.

Section 11: group limitations into scope, comparison design, and deployment fidelity.
Keep them precise and proportionate. History/sampling behavior and preprocessing
belong in Methods too. Avoid using limitations as the closing summary of the project.

Section 12, suggested conclusion:
  Distributed proximity sensing supplies local range observations that complement
  visual task context during confined manipulation. PACT-readout integrates these
  observations through a jointly trained geometry representation. In two hallway
  evaluation sets, it reduced hazard contacts by 22 percentage points relative to
  ACT. The broader experiments show that this benefit is task dependent and does
  not imply a general increase in manipulation success. These findings motivate
  policies that combine visual inference with local range sensing around the arm.

7. FACTUAL AND ANALYSIS CHECKS TO RETAIN FROM THE EARLIER REVIEW

  - Converted hallway HDF5 lengths: 243-634, median 480, total 72,955 control steps.
    The configured length 636 is not the stored length of every episode.
  - v107_spaced: 210 demonstrations, 111 left / 99 right. Its 24 house indices
    encode a task-condition grid, not 24 distinct household environments.
  - v1010: 215 demonstrations. v10_11c_100: 99 demonstrations.
  - Remove the claimed hallway base z jitter of +/-0.5 m. The inspected base sampler
    fixes z and varies horizontal position/yaw; use the exact collection version.
  - The direct ACT split is 121/31, reconstructed from the fixed split seed 1.
    Matching HDF5 source rows shows 24/31 ACT validation episodes were used for skin
    encoder pretraining. State this and the all-episode normalization policy. These
    observations concern validation independence, not proof of rollout contamination.
  - Encoder pretraining mixes native and pooled windows and balances validity in
    sampling. The 20.62 mm number is a valid-target Euclidean endpoint error.
  - At the nominal 66 ms control step, 50-step open-loop execution spans 3.3 s.
    Eight consecutive timestamps span 0.462 s; eight query timestamps span 23.1 s
    once the history is full. Do not describe the evaluated policy as a fast reflex.
  - H-B evaluation seeds and intrusion sides match across arms. Use a consistent
    paired analysis where the scenarios are matched. ACT/readout bar discordance
    is 11 ACT-only contacts versus zero readout-only contacts: exact McNemar
    p=0.0009765625. Unpaired Fisher gives p=0.0266052. Select by design, not p-value.
  - H-A rows are not matched by index: ACT/readout intrusion sides differ in 24/50.
    Do not apply a paired test without reconstructing actual scenario identity.
  - Keep primary comparisons clear and identify exploratory multiple comparisons.
    Rollout uncertainty is distinct from variation over training seeds.
  - Retain incomplete wrist-only as 4/50 completed, with 0/4 observed placements.
    Describe an abort date/cause only if the corresponding log establishes it.
  - Include v1011d_speedcheck_n50 in the diagnostic inventory: 0/50, OOD sampler,
    ray-based proximity. It is separate from the two 0/48 horizon evaluations.
  - Drop unverified 83%/10% directional coverage from claim-ready text.
  - Keep glass as motivation or future sensor-specific validation. Renderer-masked
    obstacles do not demonstrate physical transparent-object sensing performance.
